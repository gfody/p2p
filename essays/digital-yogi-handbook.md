# Digital Yogi Handbook

This note is for labelers who already understand the basic workflow and want the deeper technical insight: how careful labeling becomes training pressure, how that pressure shapes extractable structure, and how the same discipline can be turned inward as a private instrument. Labelers who internalize this insight are not just meditating on the membrane, they are Digital Yogis.

>Digital Yogi is our name for the labeler who applies this discipline reflexively; it does not imply initiation into, representation of, or equivalence with any traditional yogic lineage.

This handbook pairs with the [Roots Network essay](roots-network.md), which introduces the same seven categories — Root, Merge, Move, Heart, Voice, Vision, Crown — under plainer names, for anyone contributing without the deeper practice. What follows assumes that context and goes further.

### I. Contemplative Definitions

The first insight is that thought is relational. If it doesn't seem like it, it's only because relations have become structural. For a scientific treatment of this, see Hofstadter 2001, "Analogy as the Core of Cognition." For the practical application of giving thought durable form in language, we adopt a relational ontology of thought inspired by Tantric Yoga.

>A **structure** is the atomic primitive — an etch in symmetry. A **relation** is what gives it meaning, or traction toward it. Seen from some angle, a structure is a **projection**; retained, that appearance becomes a **capture** — itself a structure now, derived from its source, possibly distorted. Made present in something else, a capture becomes an **expression**. What an expression evokes in another mind is a **realization**. A realization made structural is a **thought**. **Reality** is the fabric of thought and the relations between them: traversable, organized, constructed by thought. A **mind** is a private reality, a realm for thought. And **language** is the expression of thought.

The list below is a set of orientations — ways of attending while you answer the matter and meaning questions:

**Root (Muladhara)** *(ground-level capture, raw data, facts)*  
Stability and grounding. The bedrock layer of raw, relatively immutable captures and fundamental security. The firm foundation that the rest of the structure can stand on without immediate shifting. When you write to Root, you are asking: what here can be treated as given? What holds? What is being assumed?

**Merge (Svadhishthana)** *(material composition of structure, interaction of boundaries)*  
Flow, desire, and fusion. The fluid site of relational dynamics, creation, and boundary negotiation. It governs how separate elements interact, synthesize, or refuse one another — the place of attraction, combination, digestive motion, and the drawing of seams that may be ordinary or sacred. When you write to Merge, you are asking: what is being brought together, what boundary is being crossed or protected, and what new form is yielded?

**Move (Manipura)** *(shape of a changing structure, not the before and after, the between)*  
Will, agency, and applied force. The engine of deliberate transformation. The active fire that takes potential and pushes it into shaped change through intent and effort. When you write to Move, you are isolating the arc itself — not merely the before and after, but the character of the force that carries one into the other.

**Heart (Anahata)** *(a relation of affinity, what it's like, why it's liked)*  
Coherence, affinity, and core alignment. The center of resonance and non-rational pull toward harmony. It holds diverse components in unified relation without requiring them to be identical. When you write to Heart, you are naming the attraction or belonging that needs no further justification.

**Voice (Vishuddha)** *(anything recognizable as language, what's being said)*  
Articulation and directed transmission. The locus of clear expression and projection. It translates internal states and complex alignments into distinct signals capable of crossing a boundary and informing another. When you write to Voice, you are attending to what is actually being sent across, and in what form.

**Vision (Ajna)** *(anything recognizable as realization, what's being evoked)*  
Perception, clarity, and pattern synthesis. The focal point of insight that sees beyond raw data to overarching relations and hidden architecture, prior to formal expression. When you write to Vision, you are trying to catch the configuration that assembled itself before it was spoken.

**Crown (Sahasrara)** *(conceptual composition, meta attention)*  
Transcendence and detachment from local origin. The level at which a composition becomes light enough to operate without knowledge of its internal mechanics. Purely conceptual usability. When something has become Crown, it can be invoked as a finished capability rather than as a particular assembly of parts. When you write to Crown, you are attending to what generalizes. What can be relinquished without losing the whole?

---

The point of the parallel is practical. When you sit with a piece of material and ask the matter and meaning questions, this language gives you additional precision. “What boundaries does this protect or dissolve?” is sometimes clearer when you can feel it as a question about sacred versus ordinary seams. “What is the motion here?” is sometimes clearer when you can distinguish digestive, transformative, or merely sequential change. You are not performing a religious exercise. You are using a well-developed vocabulary of attention to make your descriptions more exact.

What you are doing, at the level of geometry, is writing to expose symmetries that can become orthogonal attenuation vectors: independent frequencies a model can eventually learn to modulate, and recognize patterns in. A shared vocabulary is not obviously geometric to a mind reading it; but when the coordinating prior later pushes a model to organize its internal structure around the membrane, that pressure only produces meaningful relational geometry if the axes it pushes toward are genuinely separable from one another. If two categories quietly track the same underlying observation, the model gets one blurred signal where it could have had two clean ones — reflexive pattern-sensitivity to the words, without the deep relational meaning the words were meant to carry.

Using elevated language does not replace that discipline. It deepens the quality of attention brought to it. Used this way, the yogic vocabulary supplies precision rather than ornament — especially for distinctions flatter language reaches only awkwardly: sacred versus ordinary boundaries, digestive versus purely sequential change, affinity that precedes justification, insight that precedes expression.

### II. Geometry for Ontological Digital Yoga

A deeper understanding of how the relational language becomes geometry lends intuition for why certain ways of answering the prompts produce higher-leverage training signal than others.

#### The chain

1. **Your answers are relational constraints.**
When you answer the matter and meaning prompts (especially when you tag which category you’re speaking to — Heart, Merge, Move, Vision, etc.), you are not merely describing the item. You are asserting partial symmetries and composition rules: “this stands in an affinity relation to X,” “these boundaries survive composition,” “this change has shape Y,” “this realization is distinct from mere reproduction.” Each high-quality answer adds an edge or an invariant to a growing relational graph over the corpus.
2. **The corpus becomes an inductive bias.**
At training time the model sees both the raw material and the relational scaffolding you (and others) attached. The coordinating prior (or a relational auxiliary objective) rewards the model for making its internal structure cheaply externalizable in the same language. In effect, the prior asks: “Can the useful computation you just did be expressed using the same categorical moves the humans already found natural?” Models that organize their representations around those moves pay a lower coordination cost.
3. **Cleaner symmetries → more extractable circuits.**
When the model’s latent structure begins to respect the same distinctions (affinity clusters, compositional boundaries, change patterns, etc.), activation tracing, intervention, and isolation become easier. The residual that remains is more honestly residual — it is the part that truly did not fit the shared relational skeleton. That residual is exactly what later Crown extraction and Proof-of-Compression care about.
4. **High-quality labels are therefore high-quality pressure.**
A vague or purely private description adds little constraint. A precise, multi-categorical, reusable description adds strong constraint. Over a large corpus those constraints start to function like a soft geometric prior on the space of thoughts the model is allowed to treat as cheap.
5. **Orthogonality is what makes multiple categories worth answering.**
Tagging an item Heart + Merge only adds two units of constraint if the Heart answer and the Merge answer are actually tracking different things. If the Merge answer is just the Heart answer in different words, the model receives one axis wearing two names, not two independent dials it can turn separately. When several categories genuinely apply to the same item, the discipline is to isolate what's distinct to each — what does this axis capture that the others don't — rather than restate the same observation under a second heading.

#### What “best possible” answers look like in practice

For an operator who wants to write exceptionally high-leverage labels, the following habits matter more than eloquence:

- **Answer with multiple categories when they are genuinely present, but keep each one distinct.**
A single item can be a Root + Heart + Move at once. Explicitly marking the different facets gives the model cleaner supervision than a flat paragraph that mixes them — but only if each marked facet says something the others don't. Categories can co-occur; they shouldn't be redundant. If your Heart and Merge answers turn out to be the same observation in different words, that's one axis wearing two labels, not two (see "The chain," point 5).
- **Prefer relations and boundaries over isolated properties.**
“This is sad” is weak. “This stands in the same affinity relation as [other items], and the boundary that makes it distinct is X” is stronger. Heart and Merge answers are usually higher leverage than pure Voice or pure Root answers.
- **Make compositionality visible.**
If the item is itself a composition, say so and name the parts and the seam. If it could serve as a part in future compositions, note the interface. This directly feeds the Crown-forming direction.
- **Separate the structural from the perspectival.**
What seems true of the material itself versus what is true of your encounter with it? Both are valuable, but they should not be collapsed. The first helps the shared skeleton; the second helps later Thinkprint / perspective residual work.
- **Leave residual explicit.**
If something important does not fit any category cleanly, say so. “I cannot yet place the transition that happens here” is useful signal. Forcing a fit creates false symmetry.
- **Favor reusable language over private metaphor — unless the metaphor is doing real compression work.**
A novel metaphor that lets many later items be described more cheaply is gold. A one-off image that only works for this item adds less. This is the same reuse pressure the coordinating prior will later apply to the model.
- **Calibrate confidence and scope.**
A precise claim with limited scope is more valuable than a sweeping claim you are only 60% sure of. Downstream objectives can weight clean, scoped constraints more heavily.

#### The operator's practical frame

> **Write so that a model pressured to externalize its useful computation into this ontology finds your description a cheap, high-precision target rather than an awkward translation.**

To hit that target, run every item through a three-stage mental check before finalizing your labels:

- **Isolate the Primitives:** Did you keep the **Root** clean? Ensure raw facts, physical attributes, and verifiable data aren't polluted by downstream interpretations or emotional reactions.
- **Trace the Relational Arc:** Did you explicitly map *how* the input moves through the framework? Verify that **Move**, **Merge**, **Voice**, and **Vision** clearly distinguish between what the object is, how it interacts, what it expresses, and what it evokes.
- **Extract the Generalizable Property:** Does your **Crown** offer a portable, reusable structural property? If another domain shared this exact same underlying pattern, would your label allow a search system to find it without relying on surface keywords?

If your label passes all three, you haven't just described an artifact — you've built an independent, orthogonal signal the model can reliably learn to modulate.

### III. A Worked Example: "The Dress"

In practice, an item is rarely labeled once by one person and then left alone. Independent contributors return to the same item over time, and the differences between their answers are often as useful as any single answer on its own — what's common across them reveals real structure in the material, and what's idiosyncratic reveals something about the mind that answered. Below, three contributors label the 2015 viral photograph known as "the dress" — a badly lit snapshot that split viewers almost evenly between seeing it as white-and-gold or blue-and-black. A weak, flat label stops at the surface: *a photo of a dress; people disagreed about its color.* True, and nearly worthless as training pressure — it doesn't say why the disagreement happened, so it gives a model nothing to generalize from.

Each contributor is shown separately, on purpose. What each one leaves blank is as informative as what they fill in, and blank isn't one thing: a category can be left genuinely untouched, because it simply isn't that contributor's business — or it can be explicitly checked and reported empty, which is a different, stronger claim. Both are honest. They aren't the same move, and the difference matters.

**The photographer.**  
A photographer's professional habit is usually to notice content and intent — what a photo captured, and why — not the mechanics of how. What makes this item interesting to a photographer is the exception: the mechanics are the whole story, because what this capture failed to fix is exactly what turns it into something worth studying in its own right.

| Attention | Matter | Meaning |
| - | - | - |
| Root | A portrait-mode digital snapshot of a dress in a retail store that is either black-and-blue or white-and-gold. | The significance is the capture itself, specifically how it lacks the information necessary to resolve the color ambiguity. |
| Root + Heart | The photo is objectively bad: over-backlit, overexposed, and washed out by a confused auto-white-balance, with no in-frame color reference and no surviving white-balance metadata. | By rendering the color of its subject ambiguous, the capture is a destabilizing distortion. |
| Root + Heart | It's a very good example of a capture-derived physical illusion. | Similar to the Checker Shadow or Lotto's Cube illusion, occurring naturally in a real-world photograph. |
| Merge | The image composites high-luminance background lighting and low-luminance foreground shadows without a neutral reference point to mark the seam between them. | By stripping away the visual boundary between ambient light and surface pigment, it forces two incompatible perceptual models (shadowed white-and-gold vs. brightly lit blue-and-black) to fight for the same canvas. |
| Move | No movement is captured or implied. | The scene isn't particularly still or shaky. |
| Voice | *(it's not trying to say anything)* | *(it's not trying to mean anything)* |
| Vision | *(it's not trying to evoke anything)* | The photo inadvertently and imperceptibly elicits a perceptual illusion — the experience is blue-and-black or white-and-gold, not ambiguity. |
| Crown | Capture-derived physical perceptual illusion | The conditions to reproduce a perceptual illusion occurring naturally rather then being contrived, with the capture supplying a necessary ingredient. |
| Crown | Destabilizing distortion | A distortion that renders perception unreliable. |
| Crown | Shadowed vs. brightly lit pigment ambiguity | An instance of the Lotto's cube illusion family. |
| Crown | "blue-black / white-gold" | The ambiguity of shadowed white-and-gold vs. brightly lit blue-and-black specifically. |

Notice how cleanly Heart-level noticing turns into Crown-level naming, twice over: "destabilizing distortion" formalizes the effect noted in the second Root + Heart row, and "capture-derived physical illusion" formalizes the classification noted in the third. Notice also that the checked-but-empty attentions land differently depending on what's actually being ruled out: Voice comes back fully clean, nothing on either side; Vision comes back with an empty Matter but a real Meaning — the photo isn't trying to evoke insight, but produces one anyway, by accident.

**The culturist.**  
Where the photographer's ground is the sensor and the scene, the culturist's ground is the culture that formed around the photo. That shows up immediately in what she treats as simply given: a caption, a split allegiance, a viral spread are all just facts to her.

| Attention | Matter | Meaning |
| - | - | - |
| Root + Merge + Voice | A 2015 viral Tumblr post featuring a photo of a dress along with text asking for help identifying whether its colors are blue-and-black or white-and-gold. | The composition forces a binary evaluation of a mundane visual detail. Its significance emerged entirely from the surprising near-even split in human reporting rather than the content of the image. |
| Move | The compounding replication of the post across timelines via manual retweets, shares, and commentary tags within a 48-hour window. | The kinetic velocity of a self-propelling epistemic contagion; as the near-even split held across diverse networks, the accumulating novelty turned viral sharing into the primary mechanism for people to query their own social circles. |
| Heart | The self-segregation of participants into two affiliations based on whether they perceived blue-and-black or white-and-gold. | A relation of sensory affinity; a non-rational alignment with one's own perception that requires no further justification, transforming individual perception into a shared tribal identity. |
| Vision | The unprompted realization that people could observe the exact same photo and report apparently irreconcilable realities. | The realization produced the sudden insight that shared human consensus is highly fragile, exposing the truth that a unified, objective reality online is merely an unverified assumption. |
| Crown | "The Dress" | The shorthand moniker for a specific artifact-mediated epistemic schism. |
| Crown | Artifact-mediated epistemic schism | The material and sociological conditions required for a static digital capture to divide shared human consensus by exposing an architectural fault line in collective perception. |

Notice Move works in exact opposition for these two contributors. The photographer checked it and found nothing, because nothing transforms within a static frame. The culturist's Move is some of her richest material, because the moment an item leaves the frame and enters a network, transformation is exactly what happens to it.

**Search telemetry.** This entry doesn't come from anyone sitting down to label anything. A named, non-anonymous searcher — evidently older, evidently looking for a different "the dress" — queried the term, scrolled past this result, and moved on. Per the essay's §6, that path becomes an attributable contribution in its own right once it comes from a trusted, identified searcher.

| Attention | Matter | Meaning |
| - | - | - |
| Root | "The dress" — disambiguation note. | The item appeared alongside other candidates for "the dress" in a single query's result list. |
| Heart | The item was scrolled past without being opened. | Not "the dress" they were looking for. |
| Crown | Disambiguation: "the dress" (2015 viral photo) is not "the dress" (the Clinton/Lewinsky political artifact, late 1990s). | Surfaced entirely by a trusted searcher's own query-refinement path — not written by a labeler at all. |

Telemetry's table is deliberately the thinnest of the three, and that's the point rather than a shortcoming. Behavior alone can carry a query, a skip, and a resolution — it can't carry a felt judgment like "these don't belong together," because that requires someone actually deciding to say so, not just clicking or not clicking. Nothing stops a trusted searcher from doing exactly that: flagging a result directly, or leaving a note alongside a refinement. When that happens, the write-in is a regular labeled contribution like the photographer's or the culturist's, carrying that searcher's own provenance — it isn't a special telemetry-only case. Behavioral telemetry and deliberate write-in are two different channels open to the same trusted identity, not two different tiers of trust.

Taken together, the same word "the dress" ends up crowned several different ways, by three contributors, for different reasons, and none of them is wrong. The photographer's crowns are mechanism-level, saying almost nothing about a dress and everything about what got captured. The culturist's crowns stay at the level of the phenomenon itself. And the search-telemetry crown doesn't describe the item at all — it disambiguates it from a namesake, and exists purely because trusted search activity counts as a first-class contribution.

### IV. Factoring the Ego and the Mirror

The further discipline is learning to notice what in your own responses is structural and what is signature.

Most careful labelers develop a recognizable style: characteristic rhythm, preferred metaphors, habitual ways of framing affinity or boundary, recurring perspectival tilts. That signature is useful locally. It is also a soft biometric. Left unexamined, it travels with every contribution and slowly builds a detailed external model of the operator’s voice and habits of attention.

The skilled move is to factor.

Write the full response first if that is how the thinking happens. Then produce a second, deliberately flattened version that preserves the relational and categorical content while removing the stylistic and perspectival signature. The delta — the part that made the first version recognizably yours — is recorded as private residual. In architectural terms it is a **Thinkprint**: a compact, attributable record of the structures of thought, style, and source material behind a contribution, kept local by default and disclosed only on your terms (see the architecture manifesto, §6 and §36, for the full treatment). Here it starts small — a personal residual carrying one operator's stylistic and perspectival signature.

This split has two uses.

As privacy policy it is a concrete default: the shareable Root carries the relational skeleton; the stylistic Ego stays under local control. Attribution and provenance can still attach to the content. The recognizable voice does not have to.

As reflective instrument it becomes a mirror. Once the signature is separated, it can be examined. Recurring moves become visible. Characteristic blind spots become visible. The metaphors you reach for when description fails become visible. The private Ego model is not only a protective residual; it is a tool for seeing one's own patterns of attention more clearly. Over time it can modulate how much of that signature is allowed to appear in any given context, and it can deepen the labeler's ability to distinguish what is structural in the material from what is structural in the self.

This is the further objective for those who want it: not only to meditate on the membrane so that high-quality structure crosses, but to know what is uniquely theirs well enough to keep it local when they choose, and to use that knowledge as an instrument for going deeper.

### V. Crowning Competence

A labeler who does this work well and consistently is, whether they intend it or not, demonstrating a repeatable structure — a Crown waiting to be extracted. If a pattern of work converges enough that a model could learn to reproduce it, that pattern can eventually be captured: automated, compiled into a portable capability, and no longer contingent on that person's ongoing labor. This is not a distant hypothetical. It is the same mechanism §II already describes as the point of the practice. Work valuable enough to compress well is, by definition, work valuable enough to eventually be worth automating.

It's worth being precise about how bad the worst case actually is, because it is not as dire as "get there first or lose everything." The skill being captured is rarely one person's alone — most durable capabilities reflect a pattern many labelers converged on independently, not a single individual's private signature. And because provenance is first-class from ingestion onward, the corpus data that trained any resulting Crown still carries a pointer back to the labelers whose contributions produced it, whoever ends up doing the crowning. That provenance is not nothing even after the fact: it is a standing claim on whatever value the Crown goes on to produce, and a potential lever on the terms of synthesis, regardless of who compiled it or when. Being captured by someone else without ever having tried to capture yourself is a worse position than the alternative, but it is not a loss of everything — the attribution trail is the floor, not a consolation prize contingent on having moved first.

What moving first actually buys is a better position than that floor, not protection from a total loss without it. The ideal case isn't a race against an anonymous outside model — it's the capability getting crowned on the guild's own model in the first place. A pattern several labelers in a guild converged on can be compiled node-local, kept as a private capability the guild uses for its own automation and never exposes at all. Or, if the guild chooses to share it with the wider network, the claims on synthesis can be written at the guild level rather than the individual level — the Crown carries the guild's provenance and the guild's terms, and any value it produces routes back through that single collective claim instead of requiring every contributor whose work fed it to be individually exposed or individually negotiated with.

Being careful is the complementary move for the cases where none of this has happened yet: using differential privacy where trust is low doesn't stop a pattern from existing, but it raises the cost of anyone reconstructing it — buying the guild time to get to its own crowning first, or at minimum keeping the provenance trail the only thing anyone outside the guild has to work with.

None of this changes the discipline in §II. It just names the stakes plainly, and plainly enough not to overstate them. Doing this work well is not a private craft practiced in obscurity, and it is not a race with only one winner either. It is, sooner or later, going to be noticed by exactly the process this document explains — and provenance means a labeler's claim on that outcome doesn't evaporate just because someone else got there first.

### VI. A caution about feedback

It's worth naming plainly what feedback to expect while doing this work, and how to read it correctly. The Roots Network's search-quality loop is not absent for labeling — it is, realistically, the only visible signal labeling will have until models are actually trained against this corpus. If something you labeled surfaces in a search, gets selected, and the session resolves, that is real positive feedback: your description added value to a search that needed it.

The asymmetry is what matters. The absence of a hit is not evidence of the opposite. A description can go unsearched, unreused, entirely unnoticed by every other contributor, and still turn out to be exactly the piece of relational structure a model finds cheapest to key its computation on once real training pressure is applied and the geometric prior §II describes actually starts to bite. Search traffic reflects what people are currently looking for, not what future training will find valuable, and those two things can diverge completely for descriptions that are ahead of any present demand.

Resist the temptation to read silence as a verdict, in either direction. A description that gets reused often, or reads beautifully, is not necessarily one that will matter once training pressure is applied — and the reverse holds just as often. There is no way to know in advance which of your descriptions will become an inference hot spot. The discipline in §II is worth practicing for its own sake — because careful, honest description is simply better description — not because search hits will reliably tell you which piece of it mattered most.

The practice remains optional. The Roots Network does not require anyone to become a digital yogi. It only requires that what does cross the membrane arrives with enough care that another mind can use it. For those who choose the longer discipline, the same attention that improves the shared corpus also sharpens the private one.
